# Slop Valley

A playable, satirical workstation for a solo developer who has become middle management for robots. The game opens directly on three agents and an idea ready for review.

Palette: midnight #0d1220, surface #161e30, raised controls #202b42, text #eef3ff, muted text #a8b5ce, periwinkle #8392ff, ready teal #69dec0 and attention amber #f5b66e. Space Grotesk supplies expressive titles; DM Sans carries controls and prose. Fonts are bundled locally.

The memorable element is a row of colorful agent terminals: product titles lead, with agent identity, live task status and progress underneath. Beneath them the selected idea becomes a substantial workbench, adjacent to a narrow simulated social timeline. The dark sidebar holds upgrades and the founder identity. Cash, audience and attention live in the top utility bar. Ready agents have a distinct teal status; the selected terminal has a bright border and inset outline.

Desktop: sidebar | agent row, review workbench | timeline. Below 760px: sticky resources and pause control, a compact horizontal agent rail, a focused review workbench and a fixed action dock above bottom navigation. Timeline and Products have dedicated navigation entries; More holds additional controls. The dock keeps Next ready and Ship within thumb reach. The virtual keyboard hides the dock so custom feedback stays usable.

The review queue supports cycling to another ready agent and optional automatic switching after a decision. Custom drafts belong to their idea and survive agent switches. A short decision summary comes before expandable notes; feedback controls explain their effect, cash cost, attention cost and duration. Disabled actions retain a visible reason. Destructive controls use rose styling and the primary launch action uses a filled periwinkle button.

Products is the second half of the loop: persistent cards show daily income, lifetime revenue, health, and a compact revenue history. Growth, decline, death, and retirement have explicit status labels. Investment needs a free agent and can fail; retirement asks for confirmation. Concept ceilings and possible regressions make abandoning a weak idea a useful decision. One game day lasts 60 simulation seconds.

Launch posts open a discussion with five readable replies and a route back to the product. The library has 168 concepts across 12 themes and 184 contextual comment patterns. Optional local Gemini adds narrative variety after explicit enablement; the deterministic engine retains control of the economy. Distinct short sound cues distinguish decisions, ready work, income, good outcomes, and bad outcomes without requiring audio to understand the state.

Touch targets are at least 44px, mobile text input is 16px, and every interactive control has a visible keyboard focus state. Dialogs contain focus, make the background inert and restore focus on close. Reduced motion is respected. The satire stays in the work, output and reactions; visual hierarchy makes the actual decisions easy to scan.
